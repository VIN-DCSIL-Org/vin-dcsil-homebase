# Reflections

Our A1 Team Principles held up well during this high-velocity build, especially because the project moved quickly and forced us to rely on one another in real time. Conflict was never something we tried to avoid completely. Instead, we treated disagreement as part of the process. When we had different ideas about how a feature should work or how a screen should be implemented, we discussed those choices directly rather than letting them turn into silent confusion. That made the team more efficient, because we could resolve issues early and move forward with a shared direction instead of leaving uncertainty hanging over the work.

Accountability also stayed strong throughout the build. Everyone had ownership over specific features, and we made sure to follow through on what we said we would do. Because this was a fast MVP, it would have been easy for people to assume that unfinished work could just be left for later or that someone else would handle it. We did the opposite. If someone was responsible for a feature, they stayed engaged with it until it was in a usable state. At the same time, we were flexible when needed. If one of us got stuck on an implementation, we transferred the feature to someone else right away instead of letting it stall the entire project. That willingness to shift work quickly was a big part of why we were able to keep momentum.

Feedback was probably the principle that showed up most often in our daily workflow. We gave feedback through chats, pull requests, comments, and verbal conversations during working sessions. This constant feedback loop kept everyone aware of what was changing and why. It also made the work better, because people could catch problems early and suggest improvements before a small issue turned into a bigger one. In a fast build like this, feedback cannot be something that happens at the end. It has to happen continuously, while the feature is still being shaped.

Brainstorming was another place where our principles mattered. We made a point of bringing suggestions or change ideas to the group instead of changing features on our own discretion. That kept the team aligned and protected the overall design of the app. Even when someone had a strong idea, it still went through group discussion first. That helped us stay intentional, because every change was considered in the context of the whole product rather than just one person’s preference.

Looking back, the biggest lesson is that these principles were not separate from the work; they were what made the work possible. Conflict resolution kept us moving without unnecessary tension. Accountability ensured that features kept progressing. Feedback made the product stronger and prevented avoidable mistakes. Together, those habits gave us the structure we needed to build quickly without losing coordination. The build was fast, but it was not chaotic, and that difference came from the way we practiced the team principles throughout the project.
