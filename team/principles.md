# Team Principles

Our team succeeds when we communicate clearly, take ownership, and support one another with honest, constructive feedback. These principles help us work together effectively, build trust, and keep momentum even when priorities shift or disagreements arise.

## Conflict Resolution

### Prepare to Compromise
We do not approach conflict as a win-or-lose situation. When perspectives differ, we focus on shared goals and look for solutions that move the work forward without creating unnecessary friction. A good resolution balances urgency, fairness, and team impact.

### Discuss Pros and Cons
Before deciding, we pause to evaluate the trade-offs openly. We consider the benefits, risks, effort, and downstream consequences of each option. This keeps conversations grounded in reasoning rather than emotion or personal preference.

### Resolve Early and Respectfully
If tension exists, we address it early instead of allowing it to grow. We aim to be direct but respectful, keeping the conversation focused on the issue, not the individual.

## Ensuring Accountability

### Prioritize Early Communication
We communicate early when we are blocked, at risk of missing a deadline, or unsure about requirements. Timely communication allows the team to adapt, redistribute work, and avoid surprises.

### Ask for Help
No one is expected to carry uncertainty alone. If something is unclear or outside our scope, we ask for support quickly. Seeking help is a strength that improves quality and reduces avoidable delays.

### Re-Assign / Shuffle Tasks
When priorities or workload change, we reallocate work in the best interest of the team. We do not hold on to work unnecessarily when someone else can contribute more effectively. Flexibility is part of accountability.

### Own the Outcome
Each team member is responsible for the quality, completion, and follow-through of their work. Accountability means making commitments, reporting status honestly, and correcting issues before they become larger problems.

## Feedback

### Specific & Non-Personal
Feedback should be clear, relevant, and focused on the work, not the person. We describe the behavior, impact, and improvement needed without making it personal or attacking someone’s character.

### ASAP Suggestions
We give feedback as soon as it is helpful, while the context is still fresh. Immediate feedback leads to quicker improvement and prevents small issues from becoming recurring habits.

### Take Feedback on the Feedback
We are open to hearing how our feedback is received and whether it was clear, fair, or actionable. This creates a feedback loop where the team learns how to communicate better over time.

### Give Feedback to Improve, Not to Blame
Our goal is growth and better outcomes. Feedback is meant to help the team learn, improve quality, and strengthen collaboration—not to assign blame or create defensiveness.

These principles help us resolve tension quickly, stay aligned on ownership, and improve together as a team.
